/// 05 — Statistical Deep Dive

Cad Bane · Generic Vigilance Base

Full population analysis of all 112 recorded decks running this archetype — the platform's most-played build. Every comparison below is a real hypothesis test against this specific 112-deck population, not the wider field.

⚠ Read before trusting any single number here
This page ran 31 independent two-sample t-tests (one per card) at α = 0.05. Purely by chance, testing 31 hypotheses at that threshold should produce ~1.5 "significant" results with no real effect — so treat marginal p-values (0.03–0.05) with real skepticism, and weight the cards with |t| > 3 or p < 0.005 far more heavily than anything near the cutoff. Correlation here is deck-level, not causal: a card's absence often reflects a specific pilot's build philosophy, matchup-dependent sideboarding, or a weaker pilot cutting a card that's actually fine — not the card itself being weak. The regression model at the bottom in particular is exploratory, not predictive.

/// 06 — Statistical Deep Dive, Mirror-Excluded

Cad Bane · Generic Vigilance Base — No Mirrors

Same 112-deck population, but every round played against another Cad Bane / Generic Vigilance Base deck is stripped out before computing records. This isolates performance against the rest of the field from performance the archetype racks up simply by being the most popular deck and beating copies of itself.

⚠ What changed, and a new caveat this filtering introduces
142 mirror matches were excluded (19% of all 740 matches played by this archetype), affecting 79 of the 112 decks. Mean win rate moved from 50.3% → 46.5%, but standard deviation nearly doubled (19.4 → 27.9) — because stripping mirrors shrinks some decks' remaining sample size a lot. Average matches per deck fell from 6.6 to 5.3, and 5 decks now have fewer than 3 non-mirror matches (one has just 1). Those decks' win rates are close to coin-flip noise and should be weighted accordingly. As before: 31 t-tests at α=0.05 → ~1.5 false positives expected by chance.
